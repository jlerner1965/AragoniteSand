Aragonite sand for aquarium and aquaculture supply

Natural oolitic aragonite is used as substrate, graded media and a carbonate source in marine, brackish and some freshwater systems. This page sets out what is supplied, what governs suitability, and what a commercial buyer needs to qualify the material for a system or a product line.

Overview

The supplied material is calcium carbonate in the aragonite crystal form, occurring as rounded ooids of roughly 50–500 µm in the natural gradation and screened into the grades below. Representative analysis reports 96.17% CaCO₃, 1.43% strontium carbonate and silica below 0.01%, with a specific gravity of 2.93. It is a dry, mechanically processed mineral with no additives, coatings or biological content.

Buyers in this sector include distributors and brands packaging substrate lines, public aquariums and zoos, hatcheries and recirculating aquaculture operators, and system builders. Each evaluates the same three things: particle size for the bed or vessel, chemistry for the water-chemistry programme, and supply configuration for the volume involved.

Material and particle-size considerations

Suitability is decided by the system, not by the sand. The operator’s aquatic-health and operations teams set the targets; the material is selected to fit them.

Species and organism requirements
Substrate-dwelling and substrate-sifting species, life stage and stocking density determine acceptable particle size and bed behaviour.
Freshwater, brackish or marine
Aragonite is a carbonate mineral. Where it dissolves it can contribute calcium and alkalinity; whether that is wanted depends on the species and the target chemistry.
Target water chemistry
Operating ranges for pH, alkalinity, calcium, salinity and temperature are set by the responsible team. Any contribution from the substrate is condition-dependent and is not specified by AragoCor.
System design and bed depth
Bed depth, vessel geometry and whether the material is a display substrate, a deep bed or a media charge all change the size range that works.
Flow and filtration
Particle distribution affects settling, suspension, cleaning and interaction with pumps, overflows and filtration. Higher flow generally favours the coarser grade.
Operational procedures
Rinsing, conditioning, quarantine and introduction protocols are the operator’s. Natural sand carries fines from handling; initial turbidity should be expected and managed.
Applicable regulations
Biosecurity, permitting and product-labelling rules vary by jurisdiction and by whether the material is used in-house or packaged for resale.
Testing in the buyer’s system
Suitability is confirmed by a controlled small-system or isolated trial under the intended conditions, monitored before use is expanded.

Commercial aquarium applications

  • Substrate product lines. Distributors and brands packaging aragonite substrate under their own or a partner label. Grade, bag size, pallet configuration and labelling are quoted per programme; private-label options are handled through AragoCor Minerals.
  • Public aquariums and zoos. Display and holding systems that need a consistent grade in super-sack or bulk quantities, with lot documentation for procurement records.
  • System builders and service companies. Repeat supply of a known grade in bagged or palletized form.

Aquaculture-system evaluation

  • Hatcheries and recirculating systems. Evaluation as substrate or as carbonate contact media, sized for the vessel, flow and cleaning method.
  • Pond preparation and alkalinity management. The 1–3 mm WT-CAL grade is published for pond preparation and alkalinity management; rates and outcomes are set by the operator’s programme, not by AragoCor.
  • Biosecurity. The material is a dry mineral. Cleanliness controls, quarantine and introduction procedures remain the facility’s responsibility.

Questions from aquatic-system buyers

Which aragonite sand grades are supplied for aquarium and aquaculture use?

Two published grades are supplied: AG-CAL at a nominal 0.2–0.5 mm and WT-CAL at a nominal 1–3 mm. Custom classifications across the 20–325 U.S. mesh range are produced to a written specification. Chemistry is common to all grades.

Is the sand treated, coated or biologically active?

No. The material is a dry, mechanically processed mineral: collected, dried below 93 °C, screened and classified, with no calcination, chemical treatment, coating or additive. It is not a live sand product and is not sold as biologically active.

Does aragonite sand change water chemistry?

Aragonite is a calcium carbonate mineral. Dissolution can contribute calcium and alkalinity under suitable water-chemistry conditions, and the rate is system- and particle-dependent. AragoCor does not specify a pH, alkalinity or calcium outcome for any system; operators set their own targets and monitor them.

Can the material be sampled before an order?

Yes. Samples of the published grades are available on request for evaluation in the buyer’s own system. Use the quote form and select the sample option.

What documentation is available for aquatic-system procurement?

The controlled technical data sheet TDS-OA-001, grade sheets TDS-AG-001 and TDS-WT-001, a certificate of analysis for each dispatched lot, the safety data sheet, sieve analysis and source documentation are available. See Grades & Specifications.
