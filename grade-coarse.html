Fit

Where the coarse grade works, and where it is the wrong choice

Grain size should match your flow and your livestock, not your preference alone.

01

African cichlid and hard-water tanks

Rift-lake cichlids and livebearers want hard, alkaline water, and a carbonate substrate helps hold it there between water changes. The grain is large enough for diggers to move and for the bed to stay open.

02

High-flow marine and filter media

Stays down under the flow that would strip a fine bed. In a sump or reactor the larger particle keeps water moving through the media rather than channelling around it.

03

Not for a natural reef flat or for sifters

Coarse reads as gravel, not sand, and detritus falls into it where a clean-up crew cannot reach. It is wrong for sand-sifting fish and for anyone after a smooth reef bed. Take fine or medium instead.

The other two grades

Fine grade

0.5–1.0 mm · 18–35 mesh · 90 lb/ft³

The reef grade. Settles flat and suits sand-sifting livestock. Needs gentle flow at the substrate.

Medium grade

1.0–2.0 mm · 10–18 mesh · 92 lb/ft³

The forgiving grade. Holds against stronger flow and still reads as a natural flat. Start here if you are unsure.
