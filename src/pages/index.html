The material

What is aragonite sand?

Aragonite is a crystalline form of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). The sand supplied here is oolitic aragonite: naturally precipitated grains formed in warm, shallow Bahamian seawater as rounded ooids of roughly 50–500 µm, each built from concentric layers of aragonite crystals.

The supplied material is collected mechanically, then dried below 93 °C, screened and classified. There is no quarry blasting, no calcination step, no chemical treatment and no additive at any stage. Representative analysis by ASTM C 25 reports 96.17% calcium carbonate, with silica below 0.01% and a specific gravity of 2.93.

For a buyer, three things decide whether a grade fits: the particle-size range, the chemistry, and the intended use. Select material against the controlled specification and confirm it on a representative sample rather than on a product description.

Selection process

Choosing by physical requirement

A quotation is prepared against the requirement, not the other way round. These are the inputs that let a grade, a package and a freight lane be matched to an order.

  1. Intended application

    Substrate, media, blend component, top-dressing, process input. The application decides which properties matter.

  2. Nominal particle-size range

    0.2–0.5 mm, 1–3 mm, or a custom cut. State the range the application needs, not a grade name.

  3. Mesh or sieve requirement

    Where a written specification exists, share the sieve limits or target distribution. Custom classification spans 20–325 U.S. mesh.

  4. Packaging configuration

    Bagged, super sack or dry bulk, and any bag size, pallet or labelling requirement.

  5. Required quantity

    Opening order size and expected annual volume. Both affect configuration and pricing.

  6. Destination

    City, postal code or port. Freight is quoted to the named destination from Stockton, California.

  7. Documentation

    Technical data sheet, safety data sheet, lot certificate of analysis, sieve analysis or origin documentation.
