Why reef keepers ask for oolitic aragonite by name.

Round grains, not crushed shards
Ooids form as spheres in moving seawater, so the sand is naturally rounded (average sphericity 0.82, roundness 0.87). It is gentle on sand-sifting gobies, wrasses and cucumbers, and it settles into an even bed.
More soluble than calcite
Aragonite is the more soluble form of calcium carbonate, and ooids carry over 1.7 m²/g of surface area. As pH falls, a carbonate substrate can contribute some buffering. How much depends on water chemistry, grain size and system conditions, and it does not replace alkalinity or calcium dosing. Silica sand is inert.
Nothing but the mineral
Collected, washed, screened and dried below 93 °C. No calcination, no chemical treatment, no dyes, coatings or additives. Silica under 0.01%. Tested by an ISO 17025 accredited lab, with a certificate of analysis on every lot.

Questions people ask before they buy.

What is oolitic aragonite sand?

Calcium carbonate that precipitates naturally in warm, shallow Bahamian seawater as small rounded grains called ooids. Each ooid is built from concentric layers of aragonite crystals, which gives the sand a high surface area and makes it more soluble than calcite or crushed coral at the same grain size. It is the classic reef aquarium sand bed.

What is in it?

A representative analysis by an ISO 17025 accredited lab shows 96.17% calcium carbonate, 38.51% elemental calcium, 1.43% strontium carbonate, 0.24% magnesium carbonate and under 0.01% silica. The material is collected, washed, screened and dried mechanically with no chemical treatment or additives. Full numbers are in the technical data sheet (PDF, opens in a new tab), and a certificate of analysis ships with every lot.

Is aragonite better than silica sand for a saltwater tank?

For reef and marine tanks, yes. Silica sand is chemically inert and contributes nothing to water chemistry. Aragonite is calcium carbonate, so it can dissolve slowly and contribute some buffering as pH falls. How much depends on water chemistry, grain size and system conditions, and it is not a substitute for alkalinity or calcium dosing. Ours contains under 0.01% silica.

How much do I need?

Multiply the tank's length by its width by the sand depth you want, all in inches, then divide by 1728 to get cubic feet. Dry aragonite settles at roughly 90 lb per cubic foot. A 48 by 18 inch tank at 2 inches needs about 90 lb, or two 50 lb bags. The calculator above does the math.

Do I need to rinse it first?

Yes. Natural sand carries fine dust from handling. Rinse it in small batches with clean water until it runs mostly clear, then add it to the tank. Some cloudiness on the first day is normal and settles out.

Can I use it in a freshwater tank?

Only for fish that want hard, alkaline water: African Rift Lake cichlids, livebearers and brackish species. Aragonite tends to raise pH and hardness, so it is the wrong choice for soft-water fish like tetras, discus or most South American species.
